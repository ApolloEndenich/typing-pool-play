    The report

    This is an early version, and you are one of the first to finish it.

    How to play

    Bonn, 1969. Sarah Wessen is a private detective (Privatdetektivin). Kommissar Mauser of the Kriminalpolizei is how she gets through the door of a federal ministry, and each chapter is something he sends her to look into. You go where she goes, hear what she hears, and at the end you write her report to him.

    Getting about

    The map shows the building floor by floor, and on some evenings the town. Click a room to walk into it; it fills the screen. ← the map or Esc takes you back out. A small number on a room says how many people are in it.

    Time moves on, and only forward

    The bar above the map says when it is, and the button beside it moves the time on. No button moves it back. Who is in the building depends on the time: people leave, and others come in.

    In some chapters a day has two parts. During the working day the offices are full. Stay until after five ends it: the people who work there go home, the few who are about in the evening are about, and Sarah cannot go back to the afternoon. Anybody she did not get to is gone until the morning. Go home; come back tomorrow starts the next day, with everybody back at work and with time for her again, so in these chapters nothing is lost for good.

    In others the whole chapter is working days, and there is no evening to wait for. That is not a smaller chapter. What is held back there is held back by somebody rather than by the clock: a cupboard is opened for the right paper and not at the right hour, so what Sarah gets next depends on what she is already carrying. Going home and coming back still buys her more of everybody's time.

    Some chapters are a few fixed evenings instead: the bar says which, as in “evening 1 of 3”. On to the next evening leaves this one behind for good. Whoever was there only that evening cannot be found again, and what was there to be seen only that evening is not there any more. Before moving on, make sure Sarah has been everywhere and talked to everybody she wants to.

    What Sarah has already heard is never lost: the notebook keeps every evening.

    Looking

    When you walk into a room, what is there to be noticed is written under the picture. A thing in a dashed box is something Sarah can look at: click it. Most such things also lie in the picture itself, where they are: on a desk, on a counter, pinned to a wall. Clicking one there is the same as clicking its box. A thing that can be opened, like a file, opens when you click its picture. What it says is only noted once she has looked, and it goes into her notebook, where you can look at it again at any time.

    Talking

    Click somebody to talk to them. The first time Sarah goes up to a person, they will usually say something of their own accord.

    After that, you choose what Sarah does. Under Raise are the subjects she can bring up, each with the words she will use; a subject appears once something you have read or heard gives her a reason to mention it. Under Show or mention are the things in her notebook: a thing she has with her she puts in front of them, and a thing that stays where it is, like the porter's book, she tells them about. Swap, beside either heading, puts the other list first.

    People have only so much time for her. The dots beside a name are their patience, what is left of it: every answer uses one, and a question somebody cannot answer costs nothing, though it is counted: the bar above the map shows how many questions Sarah has asked and how many of them were shrugged off. Their time comes back the next day, or on the next evening. Everybody has time enough for everything they know, so nothing can be lost by asking the wrong thing.

    What you have heard

    The page has three tabs at the top. Chapters lists the chapters of the series. The building is the map and the rooms. Notebook and report collects everything Sarah has heard or seen, under the place or the person it came from, beside your notes and the report; it says how many lines are new since you last looked. The button of the same name in a room takes you there. Hide the introduction folds the chapter's opening away and leaves its question. Tick a line ✓ when you are done with it: it moves down to Done with, under the same place or person, and ↺ brings it back. ⇡ puts a line on the desk above the evenings, where it can lie beside lines from any evening; drag it by its heading to wherever you want it (a tap on the heading brings a card that lies under another to the top), close the gaps pushes every card up into the empty space and makes the desk only as big as the cards need, ⇣ puts it back in its place in the list, and ✓ is done with it from there too. ▼ beside a heading folds it away, and it opens again by itself when something new stands under it. A German word with a dotted line under it explains itself when you point at it, and so does a name.

    Select part of a line, in the notebook or on the desk, and Highlight marks it; select across a mark to take it off again. ⌇ on one card and then on another ties a string between them, and the string follows the cards wherever you drag them; a click on the string cuts it. Neither is read by the game: you still cite whole lines.

    Notes is your own; nothing in it is read or marked.

    The report

    The report is a set of sentences with blanks. Each blank takes a word that stands somewhere in what you have heard: type it in, or change the setting at the top of the report to pick words up by clicking them in the lines. When a part is filled in, Sign it. You are told whether the part holds, never which blank is wrong.

    Some reports also have a section for the people ruled out, each with their own evidence. How much evidence they ask for is the setting beside the report's other one, and it holds for every chapter:

    • Sarah's notes. Only the blanks; nobody has to be ruled out in writing. Every time you sign, it is counted.
    • Report to Mauser. The numbered parts need no evidence: their words are enough. Under a person, press ＋ cite, then ＋ beside the lines that rule them out, and sign. Whatever the parts you have signed already show about somebody counts for them by itself, so cite only the rest; sign a person even when there is nothing left to cite.
    • Mauser's file for the court. Every part cites its evidence too, and every person all of theirs: nothing counts for you by itself.

    × take out on a cited line removes it again. Kommissar Mauser marks each in pencil in the margin, and marks it again whenever what you have signed changes: a person signed early counts what you sign later. Changing the setting starts the marks afresh.

    Where people are ruled out, the result is the last thing he accepts: it stands only once everybody else has been ruled out.

    Anybody Sarah talks to may be lying to her, and so may a book, about the one who keeps it. Every case holds even so: in the end nobody is ruled out on the word of any one person. Mauser does not ask you to show that, but a report you believe should survive it.

    Telling us how it went

    This is an early version. Feedback, at the top of the page, asks a few questions about how you got on, at any point, finished or not: what stopped you is worth as much to us as how you solved it. Nothing is sent by itself. You copy what you wrote, or send it by email (to typingpool1969@gmail.com) or on GitHub, and you see everything, the play log included, before it goes. The only thing that goes anywhere by itself is a count of visits to the page (GoatCounter: no cookies, nothing about who you are).

    The pictures

    The rooms and the people are generated with AI from written descriptions, and they are drafts. The documents are made both ways: the paper, the covers and the light are generated the same way, and every ruled line and every mark on them is drawn by a small program of ours. Nothing written in any of them spells anything — the handwriting is made of pen strokes and no letters. What a picture shows is never evidence unless a line says so.

    Saving

    Where you are, what you have heard, your notes and your report are kept in this browser, for each chapter separately. Start every walk over, under Settings, sends Sarah back to the beginning and keeps your notes.

    Settings

    Forgets where Sarah has been, what she has heard and seen, and what she has asked. Your notes and your report are kept.
