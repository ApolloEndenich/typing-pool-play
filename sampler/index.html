    The report

    This is an early version, and you are one of the first to finish it.

    How to play

    Bonn, 1969. Sarah Wessen is a private detective (Privatdetektivin). Kommissar Mauser of the Kriminalpolizei is how she gets through the door of a federal ministry, and each chapter is something he sends her to look into. You go where she goes, hear what she hears, and at the end you write her report to him.

    Getting about

    The map shows the building floor by floor, and on some evenings the town. Click a room to walk into it; it fills the screen. ← the map or Esc takes you back out. A small number on a room says how many people are in it.

    The bar above the map says when it is. People come and go with the time of day: Stay until after five moves the afternoon on, and Go home; come back tomorrow starts the next day. Some chapters are a few fixed evenings instead, and once you have moved on to the next evening there is no going back.

    Looking

    When you walk into a room, what is there to be noticed is written under the picture. A thing in a dashed box is something Sarah can look at: click it. What it says is only noted once she has looked, and it goes into her notebook, where you can look at it again at any time.

    Talking

    Click somebody to talk to them. The first time Sarah goes up to a person, they will usually say something of their own accord.

    After that, you choose what Sarah does. Under Raise are the subjects she can bring up, each with the words she will use; a subject appears once something you have read or heard gives her a reason to mention it. Under Show or mention are the things in her notebook: a thing she has with her she puts in front of them, and a thing that stays where it is, like the porter's book, she tells them about.

    People have only so much time for her. The dots beside a name are their patience, what is left of it: every answer uses one, and a question somebody cannot answer costs nothing. Their time comes back the next day, or on the next evening. Everybody has time enough for everything they know, so nothing can be lost by asking the wrong thing.

    What you have heard

    The page has three tabs at the top. Chapters lists the chapters of the series. The building is the map and the rooms. Notebook and report collects everything Sarah has heard or seen, under the place or the person it came from, beside your notes and the report; it says how many lines are new since you last looked. The button of the same name in a room takes you there. Hide the introduction folds the chapter's opening away and leaves its question. Tick a line ✓ when you are done with it, and ↺ brings it back. A German word with a dotted line under it explains itself when you point at it, and so does a name.

    Notes is your own; nothing in it is read or marked.

    The report

    The report is a set of sentences with blanks. Each blank takes a word that stands somewhere in what you have heard: type it in, or change the setting at the top of the report to pick words up by clicking them in the lines. When a part is filled in, Sign it. You are told whether the part holds, never which blank is wrong.

    Some reports also ask for evidence. Press ＋ cite under a part, then ＋ beside the lines that bear it out, and sign. Kommissar Mauser marks it in pencil in the margin.

    A person ruled out by something a signed part already shows may cite that part instead: press ＋ cite under their name, then ＋ cite this part beside the part. It counts only while the part holds.

    Telling us how it went

    This is an early version. Feedback, at the top of the page, asks a few questions about how you got on, at any point, finished or not: what stopped you is worth as much to us as how you solved it. Nothing is sent by itself. You copy what you wrote, or send it by email or on GitHub, and you see everything, the play log included, before it goes.

    The pictures

    The rooms and the people are generated with AI from written descriptions, and they are drafts. What a picture shows is never evidence unless a line says so.

    Saving

    Where you are, what you have heard, your notes and your report are kept in this browser, for each chapter separately. Start every walk over, under Settings, sends Sarah back to the beginning and keeps your notes.

    Settings

    Forgets where Sarah has been, what she has heard and seen, and what she has asked. Your notes and your report are kept.

    How it went

    You are one of the first people to play this, and what you say decides what gets fixed. Answer as much or as little as you like; every question can be left empty. What you write is kept in this browser until you send it.

    A GitHub post is public and needs a GitHub account; leave out anything that would give the case away.
